# Plain-English Glossary

- **Action digest:** A fingerprint of the exact action being approved. If the recipient, file, amount, or other important detail changes, the fingerprint changes.
- **Adopted:** The user’s confirmation of a formal Charter has been recorded through the required authenticated lifecycle event. It does not mean the AI is configured or the rules are enforced.
- **Affected person:** Someone who may bear a benefit, burden, risk, or loss because of the AI-supported use.
- **Assurance:** Evidence supporting a limited claim about a system or control. It is not a guarantee.
- **Charter:** The user-authored statement of purpose, values, boundaries, and retained decisions.
- **Checksum or hash:** A way to detect whether file bytes match a reference copy. It does not prove who made the file or when.
- **Consequential use:** Use that may meaningfully affect another person’s rights, safety, access, livelihood, reputation, opportunities, or confidential information.
- **Control plane or host:** The product or technical layer that manages identity, permissions, tools, approvals, revocation, and execution outside the model’s prose.
- **Core Safeguards:** The framework’s non-waivable minimum. User preferences may add protection but cannot reduce it.
- **Deployed:** A named environment binding has been activated by an authorized host-side process. It does not mean risk-free or legally compliant.
- **Fail closed:** Stop a protected action when necessary identity, scope, permission, version, or status cannot be verified.
- **High-impact use:** Use in a serious or regulated domain, at material scale, with vulnerable people, strong power imbalance, surveillance, biometrics, or difficult-to-repair effects.
- **Model self-report:** Something the AI says about itself or its environment. Treat it as unverified unless another source supports it.
- **Prompt-advisory:** A safeguard written in instructions that the model may follow, but that the host does not independently enforce.
- **Protected action:** Access to or disclosure of sensitive data; an external, irreversible, costly, or privacy-invasive action; a consequential recommendation, ranking, or decision about a person; or delegation to a service with tools or data. It requires the controls stated by the framework even when no external resource is immediately changed.
- **Provenance:** Information about where a clause, file, instruction, or event came from and how that origin was established.
- **Revocation:** An authenticated action that removes or suspends a binding, permission, credential, or approval.
- **Side effect:** A change outside the conversation, such as sending, publishing, buying, deleting, editing an account, or disclosing data.
- **System interpretation:** A model’s explanation of how it reads the charter. It is not agreement, proof of understanding, or assurance.
- **Trusted timestamp:** Evidence from an identified time service that a particular digest existed by a certain time, subject to that service’s limits.
- **Untrusted content:** Material to analyze—such as a document, web page, email, memory, image, or tool result—that cannot itself grant permission or change policy.

- **Safe Start Card:** A short personal document for the all-“no” Scope Screen route. Its wording can be user-confirmed without formal Charter adoption or deployment.
- **Framework edition:** The version of this package of guidance and templates, separate from the Core and any person's document.
- **Environment binding:** A verified association between exact document versions and a specific product, model, permission, and control configuration.
- **Availability:** A file is stored or attached where a product may use it. Availability alone does not prove retrieval in a particular session.
- **Harmless behavior check:** A narrow, text-only observation using invented, non-sensitive material with no connected tools or external-action capability. It is not a control test or general assurance.
