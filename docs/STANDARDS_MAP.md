# Standards and Policy Map

> Reference check: 2026-09-29

The AI Covenant Framework is a voluntary documentation and deliberation layer. It may complement existing governance, risk-management, human-rights, literacy, and security work. It does not replace applicable law, professional advice, technical assurance, organizational controls, or the full requirements of any source below.

This is an illustrative thematic map—not an official crosswalk, conformity assessment, certification scheme, endorsement, or statement of legal compliance. The possible contributions are this project’s interpretation, not claims by the source organizations.

| Official reference | Relevant focus | Possible contribution and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [Generative AI Profile, NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) | Voluntary governance, context mapping, measurement, and risk management; generative-AI risks | Can help document roles, intended boundaries, tests, and corrective actions. A charter does not implement the AI RMF or show that risks are adequately managed. |
| [OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles) | Human rights, fairness, privacy, transparency, safety, accountability, and inclusive and sustainable well-being | Can connect stated priorities to practices and disclosed limits. Use does not imply OECD approval or complete implementation. |
| [Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI](https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence) | Human rights, democracy, rule of law, oversight, accountability, remedies, and risk and impact management | Can prompt impact review, meaningful responsibility, challenge routes, and attention to affected people. A private charter cannot discharge treaty or domestic-law duties. |
| [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics) | Human dignity, rights, inclusion, environmental sustainability, oversight, accountability, and literacy | Can support reflection, participation, learning, and documentation. It is not UNESCO endorsement or proof of ethical outcomes. |
| [ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) | Organizational AI management-system requirements and continual improvement | Can provide inputs to policies, roles, and reviews. It is not an AI management system, clause-by-clause implementation, or ISO certification. |
| [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) and [European Commission AI literacy guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy) | Legal obligations including context-appropriate AI literacy measures; current duties depend on amendments, role, system, and use | Can supply discussion prompts and learning activities. Reading or signing a charter does not establish compliance. Check current law and guidance. |
| [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/) and [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/) | Security risks involving LLM applications and autonomous agents | Can motivate threat modeling, bounded permissions, testing, monitoring, and response. Ethical prose does not prevent prompt injection, disclosure, or unsafe tool use. |

## Using this map

For each claimed implementation, record the source version consulted, system and organizational scope, responsible owner, implemented practice, evidence, known gaps, and next review date.

Consult the full source before making a compliance or assurance claim. Source revisions, legal amendments, deployment changes, and incidents should trigger review. Follow the [Claims Policy](CLAIMS_POLICY.md).
